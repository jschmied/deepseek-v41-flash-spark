# Verdict, 2026-09-19: where DS4.1 stands and what the best architecture is

Claims are labelled **[M]** measured here, **[P]** projected by arithmetic over measured terms, or
**[O]** open. Two [P] estimates overpromised badly today, so treat that label as a warning.

## Where we are

**6.03 tok/s** [M] — job 1010's NEW arm, 86 GB arena, `io_threads=2` + packed scales + CB3 drafter,
output byte-identical to the baseline. That configuration is **+36 %** over the previous defaults
(33.8 / 35.9 / 36.9 / 38.0 % across four interleaved rounds) [M].

A step is 525.1 ms carrying 3.17 tokens, with **88.8 expert misses** = 1,223 MB of NVMe per step [M].
At the device's measured 4.87 GB/s that is a 251 ms floor, so **12.6 tok/s is the hard device ceiling at
today's miss rate** [M+P]. We are at 48 % of it.

## Settled — do not revisit

**Format.** CB3, 3-bit codebook, **packed** scale planes, record-major, 4096-aligned. Packed scales are
+25 % capacity and bitwise [M], and they are what makes the arena slot byte-identical to the on-disk
record (13,773,312 B payload, 13,774,848 padded = 3363 x 4096) — so an expert needs **no transform
between disk and kernel** [M]. Record-major costs ~0 % on device [M].

**I/O.** O_DIRECT, one record per read, `io_threads=2`. The device saturates at **one** concurrent read:
aggregate is flat ~4.87 GB/s from n=1 to n=96 and per-read latency is 2.8 ms x n [M]. Depth buys
nothing and costs linearly. io2 is +9 % median, 3/3 paired [M].

**Cache policy.** Global LRU with `age/(1 + use count)`, +8.0 % over plain LRU in two harnesses [M].

## Dead — with the reason, so it is not re-proposed

| idea | why it is dead |
| --- | --- |
| EXL3 for the main weights | 2.9 bpw is 196 GiB for this model, 3.8 % smaller than our CB3, still TP2 [M] |
| sub-expert stripe residency | exact FFN needs every stripe, so partial residency turns free hits into perpetual partial misses: 150-370 % worse at equal budget [M]. Revive only if `miss_rate > 1 - slots/distinct`; today 0.20 vs 0.43 |
| per-layer LRU / quotas | +3.9 % equal quota, +139 % marginal-gain (starves layers to zero) [M] |
| phase-aware eviction protection | −0.0 to −0.4 % at d = 1,2,4,8 [M] |
| two-hit doorkeeper admission | −5-7 % bytes but **+16-29 % time**: an O_DIRECT read costs 9.9x the copy it avoids [M] |
| host-mapped cold pool with promotion | correct and complete, **performance-neutral** [M] — it relocated a copy instead of removing one |
| prediction in the *protection* role | perfect oracle 0.4 pp [M] |

The recurring lesson in that table: **total bytes is not the objective.** Two movements differ by 10x in
cost, and two separate ideas died by trading the cheap one for the expensive one.

## The best architecture

**One arena, allocated from CUDA VMM host-NUMA, which *is* the O_DIRECT destination — permanently.**

    NVMe --O_DIRECT--> arena slot --> kernels read it in place, forever

* real CB3 kernel **bitwise at every shape** on such an arena, **+4.5 %** at the T=6 verify shape,
  −7.6 % at T=1 [M]. The pinned pool costs +9.6 % at the same shape, so VMM is less than half.
* `preadv` accepts the range as an O_DIRECT destination [M]. 2 MiB granularity, so large-page backed —
  which retires the GPU TLB-reach question **by construction** [M].
* this **deletes** the H2D rather than relocating it. That is the whole difference from the cold pool:
  break-even is a 6-14 % miss rate against measured 9.7-30 % [P].
* **record-major is required, not preferred**: a 12-iovec `preadv` into plane-major slices is impossible
  because `w1_cb` (18,432 B) and `s1` (140,544 B) are neither 4096- nor 512-aligned [M].

It deletes the cold pool, the promotion lifecycle, `moe_forward_cold_split`, the staging buffers and the
copy streams.

**Then the part that actually matters: scheduling.** Today **nothing overlaps** — `_layer_b` computes the
routed MoE *before* the shared expert, and graph B does not launch until the host resolve finishes [M].
Reorder it (the shared expert needs no reads), then resident-first split: `moe_forward_v3_split` already
exists and is bitwise [M], and pair residency is 94-95 % [M].

**Then prefetch**, the only lever that moves the device wall. A perfect-frequency oracle is −52 % NVMe
against LRU at equal budget [M]; the corrected ceiling puts prediction at +81-84 % [M]; recall 0.30
captures 28-41 % of it [M]; our transition table sits at **30.5 % recall untrained** [M]. Run it through
the corrected scheduler (job 180) before spending training compute.

## Speed estimate

| increment | factor | note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| baseline | — | **6.03 tok/s** [M] |
| VMM arena, h2d deleted | x1.05-1.19 | h2d is 83 ms of a 525 ms step [M]; the range is how much is already overlapped |
| +4.5 % MoE kernel penalty | x0.99 | compute only, swamped |
| reorder + resident-first split | x1.10-1.25 | inferred from pair residency, **not measured** |
| prefetch at recall 0.30 | x1.23-1.34 | from the corrected ceiling |
| **compounded** | | **8.5-12.0 tok/s** [P] |

**Plan on ~9 tok/s (+50 %), not 12.** The compounding assumes independence and that is wrong: these
levers all contend for one DRAM fabric, so if reads dominate the step they cash out against the same
constraint rather than multiplying. The realistic figure sits at the low end.

Context: **12.6 tok/s** is the device wall at today's miss rate [M+P], so the upper half of that range is
already pressing on NVMe. Only prefetch moves the wall — a perfect oracle would put it at 26.3 tok/s.

**The weakest joints, in order:** (1) the h2d factor 1.05-1.19 cannot be narrowed without job 165's nsys
budget, and the standing instruction is not to use e2e brackets for decisions until it lands; (2) the
scheduling factor is inferred, and the cold-path result was precisely that temporal room is not free
bandwidth; (3) compounding independence, as above.
